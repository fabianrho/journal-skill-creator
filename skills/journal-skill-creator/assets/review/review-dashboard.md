# Visual review dashboard

Use for section or manuscript reviews and scoring under this skill. The default is a polished graphical dashboard in the conversation, with actionable paragraph details. Honour an explicit text-only request. Drafting and narrow edits do not trigger this mode.

Read [critical-review.md](critical-review.md) before constructing the assessment. Lead the accompanying review with the strongest substantive findings. Keep unresolved section-level scientific concerns visible in the editorial assessment and revision focus, even when paragraph scores are high. The separate qualitative score follows the same full-credit standard and cannot bypass a blocker or incomplete assessment.

## Assessment before presentation

Use [paragraph-readiness.md](paragraph-readiness.md) as the scoring authority. Build one assessment per paragraph: stable ID, opening phrase, location, purpose, C/E/J/F/L ratings, deductions, score or interval out of 10, coverage, verdict, reasons, next action, evidence needed and conditions for a higher score. Drive the overview and detail views from the same assessment so their numbers agree.

Keep editorial judgement, journal requirements and source verification distinguishable. Unknown evidence remains unknown; a missing source is not a demonstrated scientific defect. Preserve provisional intervals and blockers in the graphical view. Reference eligibility and section word limits belong in section checks, without repeated paragraph penalties.

## Required layout: approved three-box dashboard

This is a fixed presentation contract for every substantive scored review, including one-section and follow-up reviews. Use the bundled [HTML template](./dashboard-template.html) and [assessment data contract](./dashboard-data.md), populated through [render_review_dashboard.py](../../scripts/render_review_dashboard.py). Fill the assessment data; retain the template's structure, labels, order, spacing, score heatmap and interactions. A request to “review”, “update the review” or “make the dashboard” inherits this design. Change it only when the user explicitly requests another layout or format, or a current rendering requirement makes a technical adaptation necessary; preserve the visual structure when adapting. Narrow edits and the documented text/unavailable-surface fallbacks retain their existing scope.

1. **Top row, always above every tab:** exactly three peer boxes in order **Editorial assessment / Length / Revision focus**. Keep the row outside all tab panels so it remains present when switching views. Stack it vertically on narrow screens. “Always present” means part of each view, not a sticky or fixed overlay. Missing evidence changes the displayed status, never removes a box.
2. **Navigation:** exactly **Paragraphs / Actions / Sources**, in that order, with Paragraphs initially active. Keep this navigation beneath the boxes. Preserve selection when switching tabs.
3. **Paragraphs:** section selector with **All paragraphs**; visible C/E/J/F/L table with individual ratings and each paragraph's score/interval, verdict and coverage. Keep the legend below the table, then the selected paragraph details. The table must be visible on first render, not inside a disclosure. Retain the restrained numbered heatmap, selected ID arrow/pressed state, five dimension bars, and **Next action / Work type / Evidence needed / Score can improve** fields, plus any blocker or limitation. Keep **Why this score** and **Calculation and deductions** disclosures. Select the highest-priority paragraph initially; preserve stable IDs on updates. Use the manuscript's actual section names and score units; never replace this with an overall score alone.
4. **Actions:** ordered priorities, each with title, paragraph/location and work type, followed by **Action / Complete when / Evidence boundary**. Keep these visible in this tab; refer to the same findings used in paragraph details.
5. **Sources:** **Review coverage**, **Section and consistency checks** table, **Source register**, then expandable **Rubric v1.1 · calculation & anchors**. Retain linked source IDs, inspected sections, dates and access limitations. Keep sources and rule verification reachable in this tab even when accompanying prose discusses them.

Use a single assessment dataset for all views. The asset is a reusable layout, not a source of manuscript facts or journal limits. Preserve this contract in generated skills with local copies of the HTML asset, data contract and renderer. A new journal changes data and journal guidance, not this shell.

## Dashboard composition

- **Header:** identify the journal, article type, section, manuscript version and review date.
- **Three overview boxes:** place exactly these three summary cards above the paragraph scorecard, in this order. Arrange them side by side on desktop and stack them on narrow screens.
  1. **Editorial assessment:** a prominent score out of 10 for the reviewed text's prose and argument, a ten-segment visual, and a short reason. Label it “Qualitative editorial judgement”; it is not a paragraph-score average, evidence-complete readiness score, or acceptance probability. Keep any provisional or blocked evidence status visible. When there is insufficient text to judge, show “Not assessed” and the reason instead of inventing a score.
  2. **Length:** the measured word count against the verified applicable journal limit, a progress indicator, and “Within limit” or “Over limit”; name the section or counted scope and exclusions. For a full manuscript, use the applicable main-text count. If the limit is unverified or no single limit applies, show the count and “Limit unverified” or “No single applicable limit”, with no percentage or pass label. Never reuse example counts or another journal's limits.
  3. **Revision focus:** the highest-priority paragraph or short sequence of paragraph IDs, one concrete action, and the current evidence/review status. Derive the priority from the actual findings. If no material revision is identified, say so.
- **Paragraph scorecard:** in the Paragraphs tab below the three boxes and navigation, use a compact table with one row per paragraph. Columns are paragraph ID and purpose, C, E, J, F, L, and score / 10 with its verdict. Render integer dimension ratings as a restrained heatmap with visible numbers; show U and N/A explicitly. Print a legend with the full dimension names, weights, and 0–4 anchors. Show a fully assessed score or the provisional numeric interval and assessed-weight coverage; never substitute the interval midpoint. Make the paragraph label selectable, with the selected row visibly marked. Put the highest-priority fix in the selected detail panel; an additional action column is optional when space permits.
- **Paragraph detail:** selecting a paragraph reveals its location, why points were deducted, the smallest useful revision, missing evidence and what would justify a higher score. Start with the paragraph that most needs attention. Short expandable explanations keep the overview readable.
- **Action sequence:** prioritise concrete revisions by their consequence for the argument or scientific validity. Give each action a paragraph/location, a verb, the intended correction and an observable completion condition. Identify prose changes, source checks, study-record verification and new analysis separately; do not imply that a prose edit resolves an evidence gap.
- **Section checks and sources:** show relevant word counts and limits, unresolved requirements and source coverage. Resolve short source IDs to links, inspected sections, access dates and limitations. Make the rubric and calculation available without dominating the overview.

Visual polish should come from hierarchy, spacing, typography and useful graphics. Keep numerical bands and written verdicts consistent. Colour needs a text equivalent. Do not add ornamental metrics, predicted score gains, acceptance probabilities or manuscript-readiness averages. Completing a checklist or selecting an item does not change the reviewed manuscript or its scores; reassess revised text and evidence before updating scores.

## Rendering and verification

When the available visualize skill supports inline dashboards, use its current file, rendering and interaction contract. Keep the visual focused on scores, statuses and concise action labels; put longer reasoning in paragraph details or the accompanying review as the surface permits. Use the thread's authorized output location, without publishing or modifying the manuscript.

If inline visualization is unavailable, render the bundled standalone HTML and provide it as a downloadable file or HTML artifact. Include a concise text summary. If HTML creation or delivery is unavailable, provide the three labelled overview summaries, complete Markdown scorecard and prioritised actions. Honour explicit text-only requests. The dashboard needs no visualization plugin; its CSS and tab controls are bundled.

Before delivery, test all three tabs and confirm the overview boxes remain present in each view; check the Actions fields and Sources subsections against the fixed contract. Confirm that the three overview boxes precede the C/E/J/F/L scorecard, that the length box uses the correct count and limit, and that the revision focus matches the detailed actions. Verify displayed totals and intervals against the rubric, test paragraph selection and disclosure controls, and inspect the rendered layout for clipping or unreadable labels. Preserve the paragraph IDs and scoring version across follow-up reviews. Report an untested interaction honestly rather than claiming it works.

## Calibration

A paragraph with C=3, E=U, J=3, F=2 and L=3 has a provisional score of **5.0–8.0/10**, with **70%** of the rubric weight assessed. Display its source-verification action alongside the interval. Do not show its midpoint as an achieved score or label its upper endpoint ready. A separate qualitative overall writing assessment must remain visibly distinct from this evidence-dependent rubric result.
