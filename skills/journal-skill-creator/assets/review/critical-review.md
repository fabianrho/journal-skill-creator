# Critical scientific review

Apply this standard to substantive author-side reviews and scientific critiques. It is editorial guidance, not an official journal requirement. Assess the argument before polishing language; preserve the requested scope and the journal's own article-type rules.

## Test the scientific argument

Trace the relevant chain: clinical or disciplinary need → prior evidence → precise unresolved question → rationale for the proposed approach → objective → comparisons and endpoints → interpretation. Locate the weakest necessary connection. For a section review, inspect relevant supplied Methods, neighbouring text and the author-selected protocol when needed to judge consistency. Do not turn an introduction into a Methods section or demand information already supplied appropriately elsewhere.

- **Gap and prior work:** distinguish an evaluation gap from a demonstrated limitation of a method. An anatomical endpoint that does not answer a lesion-level question does not establish that registration performs poorly. Bound novelty and absence claims to the literature actually inspected; one illustrative paper cannot establish the state of an entire field.
- **Approach and mechanism:** require a reason the proposed method or supervision could address the stated question. For example, organ segmentation labels do not directly teach lesion correspondence. Identify what the supervision supplies and frame transfer to the target task as a hypothesis unless demonstrated. A hypothesis need not already be proven to justify a study.
- **Comparisons and attribution:** explain what the comparators establish and check whether the design isolates the claimed contribution. Separate tuning, preprocessing or search changes can make a comparison about complete approaches rather than a single component. Preserve confirmatory and descriptive roles; do not demand a new comparator without explaining which central inference requires it.
- **Endpoints and relevance:** check that the primary endpoint answers the stated objective and that clinical motivation does not become an unmeasured benefit claim. Geometric accuracy does not itself establish successful sampling, diagnostic yield or patient benefit. Distinguish primary from secondary outcomes and proposed from performed analyses.
- **Other sections:** test whether Methods can answer the objective, Results report the relevant estimates and uncertainty, and Discussion conclusions remain within the design and findings. Apply these checks only where relevant to the article type and available material.

## Findings before scores

Lead with the strongest substantive concern and its consequence, followed by concerns in priority order. Each actionable finding needs a location, the exact inferential or reporting problem, its evidence basis, the smallest useful correction, and a resolution route: prose revision, source verification, study clarification or additional analysis. Label demonstrated defects, plausible concerns requiring clarification, and unavailable evidence distinctly. A question is not proof of a flaw.

Record issues spanning paragraphs in a section-level assessment, with their contributing locations. Attribute paragraph deductions where an issue actually affects that paragraph's role; avoid repeating the same penalty throughout the section. Preserve section-level concerns in the overall verdict even if individual paragraphs score highly.

## Full-credit check

Before assigning C=4 or E=4, actively test the strongest credible objection to the central rationale or claim and resolve it against the available text and evidence. Briefly state the basis for full credit; do not manufacture objections or add arbitrary score caps.

- C cannot receive full credit when a necessary connection in the rationale is merely asserted and needs substantive explanation.
- E cannot receive full credit when novelty, attribution or clinical inference exceeds the inspected evidence. Use U when the evidence needed to judge is unavailable; use a deduction or blocker for a demonstrated mismatch under the rubric.
- Language improvements alone do not raise C or E. A request to revise “until 10/10” does not change the standard or permit invented evidence. Report what remains unresolved and what would resolve it.
- A 10/10 requires no unresolved material issue within the declared scope after these checks. It remains attainable for well-supported writing. Appropriate uncertainty, negative results and a justified hypothesis are compatible with full marks.
- If an earlier review was too generous, state that the assessment was recalibrated and explain the overlooked issue. Do not attribute a lower score to deterioration of unchanged text.

Use the existing reproducible rubric and uncertainty rules. Keep scientific judgement proportional to the evidence; preserve defensible strengths and avoid performative harshness. A section with unresolved scientific concerns cannot receive an unqualified readiness verdict through an average, a separate editorial score or a collection of high paragraph scores.
