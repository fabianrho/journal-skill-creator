# Paragraph submission readiness

Use for section or manuscript reviews, requested scoring, or submission-readiness review of text for the journal named in this skill. This is an editorial rubric created for this skill, not a journal-endorsed instrument, validated measurement, or probability of acceptance. Its weights and thresholds are explicit conventions for consistent revision priorities.

Read [critical-review.md](critical-review.md) before assigning scores. Version 1.1 retains version 1 weights and arithmetic and strengthens scientific calibration; identify this recalibration when comparing earlier reviews.

## Establish scope and sources

Identify the article type, manuscript version, section, and available study evidence. Use the default article type declared in SKILL.md only when unspecified. Read the applicable official sections in their local captures through [journal-requirements.md](journal-requirements.md) and consult [writing-style.md](writing-style.md). The bundled captures are the default basis, including for readiness checks; browse only when the user asks to refresh the requirements. State the requirements access date in every review and scorecard. Other article types retain the scoring dimensions but use their own requirements and comparable published articles.

Assign stable IDs such as Introduction P1 or Methods P3, with a short opening phrase and file/page/line location where available. Preserve paragraph boundaries; score abstract subsections and individual key points as labelled units. Read neighbouring paragraphs when available. Do not penalize a paragraph for information appropriately supplied elsewhere, or claim that an unseen section is missing. Apply opening-sentence requirements only to section openings. Track section word limits and manuscript-wide obligations once in a separate checklist.

For every judgement identify its basis:

- **Requirement:** explicit applicable journal instruction, quoted from its local capture, with URL and section.
- **Recommendation:** official recommendation, retaining its stated strength.
- **Observed style:** inspected comparable article and section; an example, not a rule.
- **Editorial judgement:** scientific reasoning or clarity advice; explain the effect on the reader.

Use short source IDs in the scorecard and resolve them to linked sources, sections, access dates, and access limitations below it. Study evidence has its own record/citation pointers. Current instructions take precedence over published examples. Never fabricate quotations or source support.

## Rubric, version 1.1 (displayed out of 10)

Rate each applicable dimension from 0 to 4 using integer ratings. Explain the chosen anchor, including why the next higher anchor is not met when below 4.

| Dimension | Weight | What earns full credit |
|---|---:|---|
| Content and paragraph purpose (C) | 25 | Fulfils its section-specific role; presents the relevant question, method, result, or interpretation with enough detail and appropriate clinical scope. |
| Evidence and scientific precision (E) | 30 | Central claims trace to inspected study records or literature; denominators, uncertainty, design and conclusions agree; interpretation stays within measured findings. |
| Journal requirements (J) | 20 | Meets the journal instructions applicable to this paragraph and location, including reporting, terminology and formatting. |
| Structure and flow (F) | 10 | Has one coherent purpose, logical sentence order and a useful connection to neighbouring paragraphs without unnecessary repetition. |
| Language and journal style (L) | 15 | Precise, concise academic English; consistent terminology and spelling; framing accessible to the journal's audience; appropriate qualification and source-supported style choices. |

Anchor meanings: **4** = no material issue found within the assessed scope; **3** = localized minor revision, meaning and support intact; **2** = substantive revision needed, but the core is usable; **1** = major reconstruction or serious scientific/communication defect; **0** = fails its intended function or contains a directly contradicted central claim. Do not reward complex vocabulary, long sentences, positive results, or a fashionable topic.

For each rated dimension, earned points = weight × rating / 4. Total = 100 × sum(earned points) / sum(applicable weights), rounded once to the nearest whole number. Keep this 100-point calculation internally; display the rounded total divided by 10, with one decimal place (80/100 becomes 8.0/10). Convert provisional interval endpoints in the same way. Preserve the rubric's possible zero rather than clamping scores to 1. Display dimension ratings out of 4 and deductions in 100-point rubric units so the result is reproducible. Assign each issue to its primary dimension; deduct in another only for a distinct explained consequence. Avoid arbitrary additional penalties or hidden caps.

Use **N/A** only when a dimension truly does not apply, with a reason; exclude its weight. Use **U (unverified)** when evidence or context needed to judge it is unavailable; retain its weight. Do not confuse lack of access with a demonstrated defect. If any dimension is U, report a provisional interval: lower bound gives unknown dimensions zero points, upper bound gives them full points. Show assessed weight as coverage. Do not normalize the known dimensions into an apparently complete high score. If no dimension is assessable, say so instead of assigning a number. Do not describe this interval as statistical uncertainty.

For partial context, rate a dimension only if all material criteria within the declared review scope can be judged. In an explicitly isolated-paragraph review, internal flow may be rated while neighbouring transitions are listed as outside scope; do not count that as full-section coverage. If missing context is necessary to judge the paragraph's purpose, use U. Rules in the bundled dated captures count as verified: rate J against them normally and cite the access date. Use U for J only when the relevant instruction is genuinely missing from the captures, was inaccessible when they were made, or is an unresolved conflict. Lack of access to study evidence alone never establishes an unsupported-inference blocker; that requires an identifiable mismatch between the inference and the supplied evidence or study design.

Readiness bands for fully assessed paragraphs:

| Displayed score / 10 | Internal score / 100 | Label |
|---|---|---|
| 9.0–10.0 | 90–100 | Ready within assessed scope |
| 7.5–8.9 | 75–89 | Minor revision |
| 5.0–7.4 | 50–74 | Substantive revision |
| 0.0–4.9 | 0–49 | Major revision |

The numeric band never overrides a **blocker**: a demonstrated central unsupported inference, contradiction, unresolved placeholder, or mandatory paragraph requirement violation. Show the score but label the verdict **Blocked**, with the exact reason. Missing evidence access or a rule genuinely missing from the bundled captures instead makes the verdict **Provisional**; if a demonstrated blocker also exists, show **Blocked; assessment incomplete**. Even 10.0/10 does not certify the manuscript or predict acceptance.

## Presentation

Use the graphical review dashboard described in [review-dashboard.md](review-dashboard.md) by default. For a text-only request or when HTML delivery is unavailable, use a compact chat scorecard with the same ratings, reasons and actions. Show ten-segment bars only for fully assessed totals; use numeric intervals and coverage for unknown dimensions. Never encode meaning in colour alone.

Overview columns: paragraph ID/opening, purpose, C/E/J/F/L ratings, score out of 10 or provisional interval, verdict, and highest-priority fix. Then give each paragraph:

1. **Why this score:** specific strengths and exact wording or locations behind each deduction, with source IDs and their basis labels. Explain every below-4 rating and every U/N/A.
2. **What to change:** the smallest useful correction and, where needed, the study evidence to obtain. Distinguish a prose repair from new analysis or unresolved author input.
3. **What would improve the score:** the rating that could change and the condition for it. Do not claim an improved score until the revised text and relevant evidence have been reassessed.

Lead with the most consequential scientific findings, including cross-paragraph issues, then present the scorecard and applicable section checks and source coverage. The dashboard's first overview box gives a separate qualitative editorial assessment of prose and argument out of 10, with its reason and scope clearly labelled; show “Not assessed” when the supplied text is insufficient. For text-only reviews, retain this as a labelled summary. It is not a rubric average or an evidence-complete readiness score; keep any provisional or blocked status alongside it. Do not average paragraph scores into a manuscript readiness verdict: an excellent average can conceal a critical defect. On repeated reviews retain IDs and rubric version and explain score changes against the previous text. If boundaries change, map old to new IDs.

## Calibration examples (synthetic; not study results)

- Ratings C=3, E=4, J=3, F=2, L=3 earn 18.75+30+15+5+11.25 = 80 points, displayed as **8.0/10**, minor revision if no blocker. Explain each deduction; a number alone is insufficient.
- The same ratings with E=U yield **5.0–8.0/10 provisional** (50–80/100 internally), 70% of applicable weight assessed. Request the missing evidence; do not treat the upper bound as achieved readiness.
- A fluent conclusion that declares equivalence solely from a nonsignificant comparison has a demonstrated scientific blocker regardless of its language rating. Explain the inferential problem as editorial/scientific judgement, not a fabricated journal quotation.
- An isolated middle Methods paragraph should not lose points for a statement required only at the section opening. An unseen section opening is a context limitation, not evidence of noncompliance.
- A narrative review should not lose points for an unstructured abstract when its own article-type instructions allow it.

- **Fluent but incomplete rationale:** a paragraph asserts that organ segmentation adaptation will improve lesion matching without explaining the transfer. When this missing connection requires substantive revision, C=2 is appropriate even if L=4. If E=J=F=4 after verification, the reproducible total is 8.8/10 (numeric band: Minor revision); explicitly retain the substantive C concern. This omission alone does not establish a scientific blocker; apply the blocker rule only when its criteria are met. Do not infer that the method fails.
- **Supported full credit:** a paragraph identifies a bounded gap, explains a plausible approach, explicitly frames unproven transfer as the study question, and aligns its objective with the supplied comparisons and endpoint. If all five dimensions have been assessed and no material issue remains, 10.0/10 is justified.
- **Appropriate uncertainty:** “Whether this representation supports lesion localisation remains to be tested” can be precise scientific framing. Do not deduct merely because the study asks an unanswered question; distinguish that uncertainty from unavailable evidence needed to check a factual assertion.
- **Attribution mismatch:** a stated isolated effect of adaptation conflicts with supplied Methods that change both the encoder and matching optimisation. Narrow the claim to comparison of the complete approaches or explain the analysis needed to isolate the effect; elegant wording does not resolve the mismatch.
